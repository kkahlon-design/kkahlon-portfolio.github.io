UX Design Institute diploma

Airline booking redesign

A nine-month diploma project taking an airline booking flow from usability research and affinity mapping through to a high-fidelity prototype, then a full UI redesign.

My role
User research, research analysis, wireframing, prototyping
Duration
September 2023 to June 2024
Tools
Figma, Miro, Google Forms
01

Task

What's the task?

On the UX Design Institute course I was tasked with developing a new solution for an airline booking website. The curriculum was broken into individual projects, each submitted for marking.

Aims and objectives

The goal was to create a high-fidelity prototype following the UX design process in the curriculum. By the end I'd have the foundational knowledge to apply these principles in real-world scenarios, and a project for my portfolio.

02

Research

Competitive benchmarking

I researched a range of competitor products to understand user flows, mental models and visual hierarchy. I chose airlines at different price points, which helped me identify key features and pain points across the market.

Online survey

Using Google Forms, I created a survey to gather qualitative and quantitative data from friends and family about past experiences on airline websites. Open-ended questions like ‘Why did you visit the website that day?’ and ‘Were you able to complete your tasks? If not, why?’ gave empathetic insight into user needs and pain points.

Note taking

I was given two recordings of usability tests, each showing a user on two different airline websites. Watching them, I found patterns in behaviour and decision-making. Both users showed how conventions shaped their interactions.

Usability testing

I ran usability tests with real users (with no UX knowledge) to observe how they booked flights on these platforms, taking detailed notes of observations and quotes.

As well as building soft skills like communication and empathy, this gave me a primary source of data to collate and analyse in the next phases.

03

Analysis

Affinity diagram

I used Miro to create an affinity diagram, making several passes to organise ideas into groups and subgroups. This isolated the key patterns where I could identify recurring pain points.

Customer journey map

Next, I created a customer journey map to visualise the experience across each stage of booking. Using the screens as stages, I mapped touchpoints, actions, pain points and opportunities with insights from the research phase.

The customer journey map was unfortunately lost from the Miro board during the project. The affinity diagrams above represent the analysis that directly informed the flow diagrams in the concept phase.

04

Concept

Flow diagrams

In the concept phase I created a diagram of the main user flow, from the moment someone opens the website through every step needed to book a flight. Different shapes represent screen content, processes and decisions.

Interaction sketches

Using the flow diagram as a foundation, I conceptualised the interactions for each screen. I started with low-fidelity wireframes and paper cut-outs, which saved time and let me experiment with layouts. I then noted the features on each screen with a short description of how they work.

05

Design

Prototype

Using the wireframes as a guide, I built an interactive prototype in Figma. Learning new software had its challenges, but I persevered and became proficient enough to create a functional prototype. Visual hierarchy and user feedback shaped the design.

One area I know I could have improved was the UI. I first designed a very simple interface with minimal styling so I could focus on the problem at hand. Even so, my tutors gave a lot of positive feedback on the usability and overall experience.

Usability testing

To stay user-centred, I ran further usability tests to validate design decisions and gather constructive feedback. The feedback was largely positive: users had little friction navigating the booking process and valued how streamlined it felt.

Annotations

The final project focused on handoff. I learnt how specific, detailed annotations reduce the risk of errors in development. Using Figma's comments, I annotated the prototype with design rationale and how each element should respond to the user.

Redesign

After the course I revisited the design with fresh eyes and a stronger foundation in UI. I rebuilt the interface from scratch in Figma: darker, more immersive and visually cohesive. The booking flow was refined, the navigation made more intuitive, and the visual language brought closer to industry standards.

06

Reflection

What would I do differently?

With more time, I'd have researched UI design more extensively. It would have improved the overall quality and let me explore more innovative solutions. At the time I focused on principles like Hick's Law and visual affordances to guide users through booking, keeping myself focused so I'd meet the curriculum's targets.

My lack of Figma experience also limited the designs. It was my first time using it, but with repetition I learnt features like auto layout and components, which let me redesign the UI and vastly improve how the site looks and feels.

What's next?

I plan to keep experimenting in Figma to push my design skills further. I'm a logical thinker, so I prioritise data-driven decisions. I want to build on the foundation from the course by studying a wider range of successful products and analysing why their design decisions work so well.
